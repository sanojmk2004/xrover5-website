Every candle on a gold chart is a small story of the battle between buyers and sellers. Learn to read those stories and you can make much better decisions than by relying on lagging indicators alone. This guide focuses on the candle concepts that matter most on XAUUSD.

Anatomy of a candle

  • Body: the distance between the open and the close. A large green body shows strong buying; a large red body shows strong selling.
  • Wicks (shadows): the high and low reached during the candle. Long wicks show that price went somewhere and was pushed back.
  • Close: the most important price of the candle. A candle isn't "finished" until it closes, and its meaning can change completely in the last seconds.

Context first, pattern second

A pattern in the middle of nowhere means very little. The same candle becomes meaningful when it forms at an important level: a previous high or low, a support or resistance zone, a session high or low, or a round number like 4,000 or 4,050. Gold respects round numbers more than many instruments.

Five candle patterns worth knowing on gold

1. Rejection candles (pin bars)

A candle with a long wick and a small body shows that price tried to push in one direction and failed. A long lower wick at support suggests buyers stepped in; a long upper wick at resistance suggests sellers defended the level. On XAUUSD these often form at session highs and lows.

2. Engulfing candles

A bullish engulfing candle has a body that completely covers the previous red candle's body, a sign that buyers have taken control. A bearish engulfing is the opposite. They are strongest after a clear move into a key zone.

3. Inside bars

An inside bar is a candle that forms entirely within the range of the previous candle. It signals a pause or compression. Gold often breaks out strongly from inside bars, especially before the London or New York open.

4. Momentum (marubozu) candles

A large body with very small wicks shows one side in complete control. A momentum candle breaking through a level is often more convincing than a slow drift.

5. Doji and indecision candles

A candle whose open and close are almost equal shows balance. After a long trend into a key level, a doji can warn that the move is losing strength. On its own it is not a signal to trade.

Why confirmation matters so much on XAUUSD

Gold is fast. Wicks can stretch several dollars beyond a level and snap back, catching traders who entered too early. A few habits help:

  • Wait for the candle to close before acting on a pattern.
  • Look for follow-through from the next candle in the expected direction.
  • Check a higher timeframe so you are not buying a perfect 1-minute pattern straight into a 1-hour resistance.
  • Know where you're wrong. Place your stop beyond the wick or zone that invalidates the idea, then size your position to fit.

Common mistakes

  • Trading every pattern you see instead of only those at meaningful levels.
  • Ignoring the trend and repeatedly trying to catch tops and bottoms.
  • Entering mid-candle, then watching it close as a completely different pattern.
  • Trading candle patterns into high-impact news, where normal behaviour breaks down.
