Every trade you open has a cost. On gold (XAUUSD) that cost is mainly the spread, sometimes a commission, and swap if you hold a position overnight. A forex rebate, also called forex cashback or an IB rebate, gives you back a small part of that cost for every lot you trade. This guide explains where the money comes from, how a rebate program works step by step, what it can and cannot do for you, and how to check a program before you join.

First, what is an introducing broker (IB)?

An introducing broker (IB) is a person or company that introduces new clients to a broker. The IB does not hold your money and does not execute your trades. Your account, your deposits and your withdrawals stay with the broker.

In return for bringing in clients, the broker pays the IB a commission. This is usually calculated on the trading volume of the clients the IB introduced, for example a fixed amount per lot traded or a share of the spread or commission the broker earns. The exact terms are agreed privately between the broker and the IB.

A rebate program is simply an IB that shares part of this commission back with the traders it introduced. That shared part is your rebate.

Where does the rebate money come from?

This is the most important point to understand: the rebate does not come from your trading profits and it is not an extra charge on top of your costs. It comes out of the commission the broker already pays the IB.

In a genuine program, your spreads and commissions are the broker's normal ones for that account type. You pay the same trading costs you would pay without the rebate, and part of the broker's IB payment comes back to you. That is why a rebate works like a discount on your trading costs.

How a rebate program works, step by step

Programs differ in detail, but most follow the same pattern. Here is how the XDemat IB Rebate Program works:

  1. Register for the rebate program. Joining is free.
  2. Open an MT4 or MT5 account with a partner broker through the program's IB link. The link is what tells the broker that your account belongs under that IB. Only accounts opened through the link qualify.
  3. Connect the account with its read-only investor password. This lets the program see how many lots you traded. Investor access can view trades only; it cannot place trades or withdraw money.
  4. Trade as you normally would. The broker records your volume and pays its IB commission to the IB on its own schedule.
  5. Receive your share. When the broker pays the IB commission, your share is added to your rebate balance.
  6. Request a payout. Once your balance is above the program's minimum, you request a payout in USDT (TRC20 or BEP20) to the wallet address you added.

A worked example (illustrative numbers)

The numbers below are only for illustration. They are not the rates of any broker or program, and real spreads change all the time.

On XAUUSD, one standard lot is 100 ounces of gold. If the spread were $0.20 per ounce, the spread cost of one standard lot would be 100 × $0.20 = $20.

Spread cost per lot = contract size × spread = 100 × $0.20 = $20

Now suppose, for example, a rebate were $2 per lot. Your net cost for that lot would be $20 − $2 = $18, which is 10% lower. If you traded 15 lots in a month, the rebate would be 15 × $2 = $30.

What a rebate can and cannot do

A rebate can:

  • lower your real cost per trade, which matters most for traders who trade often, such as scalpers and intraday traders;
  • give you back part of a cost you would pay anyway, whether a trade wins or loses.

A rebate cannot:

  • make a losing strategy profitable;
  • reduce your trading risk in any way;
  • replace a stop-loss, sensible position sizing or a trading plan.
Important: Never trade more, bigger or more often just to earn rebates. Extra trades bring extra risk and extra costs, and the rebate is only a small part of those costs. Trade your normal plan and treat the rebate as a discount, not as income.

Rebate vs trading bonus

People sometimes confuse rebates with bonuses. They are different. A rebate is a share of commission based on the lots you actually traded. A bonus is usually credit that a broker adds to an account, often with conditions, such as a minimum trading volume before it can be withdrawn. Always read the terms of any offer so you know exactly what you are receiving.

How to check a rebate program before you join

  • It should be free. You should not need to pay a joining fee.
  • Your trading costs should stay normal. Ask whether your spread or commission is the broker's standard one for your account type.
  • Give the investor password only. The read-only investor password is enough to count lots. Never share your main trading password or your broker client-area login with anyone.
  • Know which accounts qualify. Usually only accounts opened through the IB's link qualify. An account you already have directly with a broker normally does not, unless the broker agrees to move it.
  • Understand the payout rules. Check the payout method, the minimum payout and when rebates are confirmed. These can change, so read the current terms on the program's own page.
  • Check local rules. Make sure receiving crypto payouts such as USDT is allowed where you live, and remember that rebates may be taxable income in your country.

Who benefits most from rebates?

Because the rebate is paid per lot, the total depends on how much you trade. A trader who opens a few small positions a month will receive a small amount. A trader who already trades actively, with tested rules and controlled risk, may see the rebate make a noticeable difference to total costs over a year. In both cases the rebate is a cost saving, not a reason to change how you trade.

Frequently asked questions

What is a forex rebate?

A forex rebate, also called forex cashback, is a part of the commission a broker pays an introducing broker (IB) that the IB shares back with the trader. It is usually paid per lot traded and works like a discount on trading costs.

Does a forex rebate increase my spread or trading costs?

In a genuine program, no. Your spreads and commissions are the broker's normal ones for your account type, and the rebate comes out of the commission the broker already pays the IB.

Can a rebate make a losing strategy profitable?

No. A rebate only lowers your trading costs. It does not change the result of your trades or your trading risk, so you should never trade more just to earn rebates.

Is it safe to give my investor password for rebates?

The investor password gives read-only access. It can view trades but cannot place trades or withdraw money. Never share your main trading password or your broker client-area login.
