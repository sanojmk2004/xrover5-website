Gold rewards traders who are disciplined and punishes those who are not. The difference between traders who last and traders who quit after a few months is rarely their entry strategy. It is almost always risk management. Here are nine rules every XAUUSD trader should follow.

1. Risk a small, fixed percentage per trade

Decide in advance what percentage of your account you will risk on each trade. Many experienced traders use 0.5% to 1%, and rarely more than 2%. At 1% risk, even a run of ten losing trades in a row leaves around 90% of your capital, enough to recover. At 10% risk, the same streak can wipe out most of the account.

2. Always use a stop-loss

Gold can move tens of dollars in minutes. A trade without a stop-loss is an open-ended risk. Place your stop when you enter, at a level where your trade idea is clearly proven wrong.

3. Size the position from the stop, not the other way round

Choose your stop based on market structure, then calculate the lot size that keeps your loss within your fixed risk. Our position sizing guide shows the exact formula.

4. Never widen a stop-loss

Moving your stop further away when price approaches it turns a small planned loss into a large unplanned one. You can move a stop to reduce risk or lock in profit, but never to give a losing trade "more room".

5. Respect high-impact news

Around CPI, Non-Farm Payrolls and Fed decisions, gold can spike in both directions, spreads widen and stops can fill with slippage. Unless you trade a tested news strategy, consider closing or reducing positions beforehand and waiting for the market to settle afterwards. See what moves gold prices for the events to watch.

6. Account for spread and slippage

Spreads on XAUUSD are usually tight during the London and New York sessions but can widen sharply during quiet hours, around the daily rollover and at news times. Leave a small buffer in your stop placement and avoid entering when spreads are unusually wide.

7. Set a daily loss limit

Decide on a maximum loss for the day, for example 2% to 3% of your account. When you hit it, stop trading until the next session. This single rule prevents "revenge trading", where traders try to win back losses quickly and make things far worse.

8. Aim for a sensible reward compared with risk

Look for setups where the potential reward is at least as large as the risk, and ideally larger. Taking partial profits at a first target and letting the rest run to further targets is a common way to balance winning rate and reward. Also be honest about your results: a high win rate means little if a few big losses erase many small gains.

9. Practise first, then keep a journal

Trade XAUUSD on a demo account until you can follow your rules consistently. When you move to live trading, start with the smallest size your broker allows. Record every trade (entry, stop, target, result and the reason you took it) and review it weekly. Your journal will show you which sessions, setups and habits are helping or hurting you.

Leverage is a tool, not a target

Brokers may offer very high leverage. That only changes the margin needed to open a trade, not how much you should risk. Overleveraging is the fastest way to lose an account on a volatile instrument like gold. Let your risk rules decide position size, every time.
