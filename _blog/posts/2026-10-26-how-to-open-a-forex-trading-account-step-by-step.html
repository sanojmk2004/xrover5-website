Opening a forex trading account is not difficult, but small mistakes at the start can cause big problems later: a withdrawal that is blocked because a document is missing, an account opened with the wrong company, or real money traded before you understand the platform. This guide walks you through the process in order, from checking the broker to your first small deposit. It is general and educational; exact screens and rules differ from broker to broker.

Step 1: Check the broker before you sign up

Do this before you type in any personal details. Give your money and identity documents only to a firm you have checked yourself.

  • Licence: look up the firm on the public register of a financial regulator, such as the FCA (United Kingdom), ASIC (Australia), CySEC (Cyprus) or FSCA (South Africa). Do not trust a licence number shown only on the broker's own website.
  • Company name and website: the legal company name and web address on the register should match the ones you are using. Fake "clone" websites copy real firms.
  • Costs and conditions: spreads, commissions, swaps and withdrawal rules for gold (XAUUSD).

Step 2: Register and secure your login

Registration usually starts with your email address, phone number, country of residence and a password. A few points matter here:

  • Use your real legal name, exactly as it appears on your passport or national ID. If the names do not match, verification will fail or withdrawals may be refused later.
  • Choose your true country of residence. It decides which company of the broker group opens your account and which rules and protections apply to you.
  • Use a strong, unique password and turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) if the broker offers it. Your client area controls deposits and withdrawals, so protect it like a bank account.
  • Read the documents you are asked to accept: the client agreement, the risk disclosure and the privacy policy. Note the legal company name in the client agreement.

Step 3: Complete KYC verification

KYC means "Know Your Customer". Regulated brokers must confirm who you are and where you live before they let you trade or withdraw.

Most brokers ask for two documents:

Proof of identityPassport, national ID card, driving licenceValid (not expired), all four corners visible, clear photo, both sides of a card if asked
Proof of addressUtility bill, bank statement, official letter showing your name and addressUsually must be recent (often within the last 3 to 6 months) and show the same name and address as your account

Some brokers also ask for a selfie or a short video check, and some ask for a photo of the card you use to deposit (with most of the card number covered). You may also be asked about your job, income, source of funds and trading experience. Answer honestly. These questions help the broker decide whether leveraged products like CFDs are appropriate for you, and false answers can lead to the account being closed.

Verification can take from a few minutes to a few working days. Upload clear files the first time to avoid delays. Do not send documents by chat or email to people who contact you claiming to be from the broker; upload them only inside the official client area.

Step 4: Choose the account settings

When you open the trading account itself (often called a "live account"), you will usually choose a few settings. Some cannot be changed later without opening a new account.

  • Platform: MT4 or MT5 are the most common for gold. Choose the one your tools and trading software need.
  • Account type: brokers often offer a standard account (wider spread, no commission) and a raw or zero-spread account (tight spread plus a commission per lot). Compare the total cost for your normal trade size.
  • Base currency: the currency your balance is shown in. A USD account is simple for gold, because XAUUSD profits and losses are calculated in US dollars.
  • Leverage: higher leverage only means less margin is needed; it does not make trading safer. Many regulators cap retail leverage on gold (for example, 1:20 under UK and Australian retail rules). Choose your position size by risk, as shown in our gold risk management rules.

After the account is created, the broker sends your login details: the account number, the trading password, the server name and often an investor (read-only) password. Store them safely. The investor password lets someone view the account but not place trades.

Step 5: Practise on a demo account first

A demo account uses virtual money with real market prices. Open one on the same platform and, if possible, the same account type as your live account. Use it to learn the practical things that cause costly mistakes with real money:

  • How to place market orders, pending orders, stop-loss and take-profit levels.
  • How to check the XAUUSD contract size in the symbol details. On most platforms 1 lot = 100 ounces, but always confirm it.
  • How a price move turns into profit or loss for your lot size. Our lot size and pip value guide shows the calculation.
  • How spreads behave in quiet hours and around major news.

Remember that demo results do not prove you will make money live. Demo fills can be smoother than real fills, and trading real money feels very different. Treat the demo as training, not as a forecast.

Step 6: Make a small first deposit

Once your account is verified, go to the deposit page in the client area. Before you pay:

  • Pay from an account in your own name. Most brokers refuse third-party payments, and withdrawals usually go back to the same method you used to deposit.
  • Check fees and conversion. Look for deposit fees, the exchange rate if your money is in another currency, and the processing time.
  • Pay only through the official client area. Never send money to a personal bank account or personal wallet because someone asked you to.
  • Start small. Deposit only money you can afford to lose. You can add more later after you have tested the account.
  • Be careful with bonuses. Some deposit bonuses come with conditions that restrict withdrawals until you trade a large volume. Read the terms or decline the bonus.

Step 7: Place a small trade and test a withdrawal

Log in to the platform with your live account number, password and server. Check that the balance matches your deposit. Place one or two very small trades with a stop-loss, so you see real spreads and real execution.

Then request a small withdrawal. This is the most useful test of a new broker. Note how long it takes, whether any fee appears, and whether support answers your questions clearly.

Common mistakes when opening an account

  • Signing up through a link from a stranger on social media without checking the website on the regulator's register.
  • Choosing the maximum leverage without understanding margin and stop-out levels.
  • Skipping the demo and learning the platform with real money.
  • Depositing a large amount before testing a withdrawal.
  • Sharing the trading password (not the investor password) with someone who offers to "manage" the account.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need to open a forex trading account?

Most brokers ask for a proof of identity, such as a passport, national ID card or driving licence, and a recent proof of address, such as a utility bill or bank statement. Some also ask for a selfie or a photo of the card you use to deposit.

How long does forex account verification take?

It can take from a few minutes to a few working days, depending on the broker and the quality of your documents. Clear, valid documents with your full legal name usually make it faster.

Should I use a demo account before trading gold live?

Yes. A demo account lets you learn orders, stop-loss levels, contract size and spreads without risking money. Demo results do not guarantee live results, so treat it as training.

Can I deposit money from someone else's bank account?

Usually not. Most brokers refuse third-party payments and send withdrawals back to the method you deposited with, so pay from an account in your own name.
